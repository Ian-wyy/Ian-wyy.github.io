Yiyu Wang

I'm an undergraduate student at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (B.S. in Mechanical Engineering) and at the University of Michigan, Ann Arbor (B.S. in Computer Science, second bachelor's). I work in the Robotics Autonomy and Planning Lab with Prof. Zhongqiang (Richard) Ren, in the Scalable Spatial Intelligence Lab with Prof. Yulun Tian, and in the Advanced Agent-Robotics Technology Lab at Carnegie Mellon University with Prof. Katia Sycara, where I also collaborate with Prof. Yaqi Xie and Bowen Li.

I work on planning and agentic systems for robots. I am interested in how embodied agents can carry out long-horizon tasks: planning over what they know, learning and refining their own skills, and keeping a memory of the world they act in. Before this, I worked on search-based planning for heterogeneous multi-robot systems.

I am actively looking for research internships, and for a Ph.D. position in robotics, computer science, or related fields starting in Fall 2027.

Research

My earlier work used heuristic search to plan routes and exploration for teams of physically different robots.

Publications
Papers where I am a (co-)first author are highlighted.

Algorithms and Solvers for Min-Max Constrained Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Routing Problems
Yiyu Wang, Yunxiang Yao, Shizhe Zhao, Zhongqiang Ren
IROS, 2026
paper to be released / package website / solver demo

An anytime focal-search solver for min-max heterogeneous multi-robot routing under capacity, resource, and time-window constraints, implemented in C++ and released as the PyRCH Python package.

HEHA: Bounded Suboptimal Planning for Constrained Routing and Its Use in Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Exploration of Unknown Environments
Longrui Yang†, Yiyu Wang†, Jingfan Tang†, Yunpeng Lv, Shizhe Zhao, Chao Cao, Zhongqiang Ren
† Equal contribution
IEEE T-RO, conditionally accepted, 2026
MRS Poster Paper, 2025
paper / demo

Path planning for autonomous exploration of an unknown environment by drones, wheeled, and legged robots with different terrain-traversal capabilities. HEHA splits the problem into global routing, solved by a bounded-suboptimal anytime focal search that minimizes the makespan under traversability constraints, and a heterogeneity-aware local planner that avoids duplicated exploration.
